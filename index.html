The three weeks

Three weeks. Six live sessions.

Nine topics, two live sessions a week. Every week pairs the practical build work with the money side and the personal side — because in practice they're the same problem. Research on first-time founders keeps finding the same two killers: building something before checking anyone wants it, and running out of focus or energy before it lands. We designed the three weeks around both.

Why now

"Who am I to build
something like this?"

You see what AI and technology are doing to communities that were never asked. You've had the idea for the thing that would help. And then that sentence arrives.

The people deciding what technology does to all of us are mostly people who simply began. You already have the part that can't be shortcut — you understand the problem. The rest is a set of skills, and that's what these three weeks are.

Who it's for

If one of these is yours, come.

›"Who am I to do something like this?"

The sentence we hear most. It doesn't dissolve through pep talk — it dissolves when five people tell you the problem is real and someone uses the thing you made.

›"I only have a problem, not an idea"

Perfect. That's where everyone starts here, including people who arrive with a finished plan.

›"I've never built anything"

Neither had most people whose products you use. You bring years of understanding a real problem. We bring the making.

›"I've been researching this for two years"

And still not started. We'll put a gentle deadline and a group of women around you, and you'll make the decision you've been carrying.

›"I'm afraid it won't be any good"

It probably won't be, first time. That's the design. First versions are meant to be rough — that's how they teach you anything.

Common questions

Before you talk yourself out of it.

›I don't have an idea yet. Can I still come?

Yes, and it's the better starting point. Bring a problem you keep noticing — in your field, your community, your own life.

›What kind of thing can I build?

A blog, a newsletter, a podcast, a community, a campaign, a course, a service, a tool, a company. If it responds to a problem and other people will meet it, it counts.

›Do you cover how to make money from it?

Yes. Week 3 is business models, pricing, funding and grants — how a project like yours could earn, and who would pay for it. Plenty of people arrive wanting to get paid for work they actually care about, and we treat that as a legitimate goal rather than a sell-out.

›Do I need technical skills?

No. Most first versions here aren't software at all. When yours needs to be, we'll show you what to use and what you can fake.

›Will it be finished at the end?

No — three weeks gets you a real first version and a clear sense of what it needs next. That's a much better place to stand than three more weeks of planning.

›What if it doesn't work?

Then you found out in three weeks, surrounded by people who were rooting for you. Changing direction is a result, not a failure, and we treat it that way.

›I have a full-time job. Is this realistic?

It's built for people who do. Sessions sit outside standard working hours, and the work between them is around three to four hours a week.

›Is it only for women?

The cohort is for women, including trans women, and for non-binary people who find the space relevant.
